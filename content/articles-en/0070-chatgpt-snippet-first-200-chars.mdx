## Conclusion

We ran five prompts through ChatGPT and pulled **383** search-result snippets from the conversation JSON. Every one was **200–206 characters** long, **100% were truncated at the end**, and on all six pages we checked, **not a single character of the meta description appeared**.

So what is being cut? We fetched the actual pages and compared them. On **all 12 pages we could match, boilerplate such as the header, global navigation and "Skip to content" had been discarded**. It isn't a mechanical 200 characters from the top of the page; it's **the first 200 characters of the extracted body text**.

When the same URL is picked up by a different query, the snippet doesn't change by a single character. Across days, however, 18% changed, and in some cases **body extraction failed and navigation filled the slot**.

In short, what ChatGPT receives is **the first 200 characters of your body text**. What lands there shapes how your company gets introduced.

All lengths in this article are character counts, not word counts, taken from a Japanese-language study whose sample includes both Japanese and English pages.

## What we looked at

ChatGPT's conversation JSON records the search queries the model issued and the URLs, titles and snippets that came back. This article deals with the `search_result_groups[].entries[].snippet` field.

| What we looked at | Where it is in the JSON |
| --- | --- |
| Search queries generated by the model | `content.text` of messages whose `recipient` is `web.run` |
| Candidates returned by search | `url` / `title` / `snippet` in `metadata.search_result_groups[].entries[]` |
| URLs cited in the answer | `metadata.content_references[]` of the final message |

Conditions:

- Logged in, desktop web, model `gpt-5-6`
- Memory off, every custom instruction field empty
- New chat each time; JSON captured only after the answer finished
- Five prompts about SEO and GEO tools, run on two days

**First, what the logs don't contain.** The exact string the model received is not recorded. The JSON `snippet` is a display value truncated with `…`. When the model fetches a page's body, that body isn't logged either. Everything observed here is limited to what the client side records.

## ChatGPT cuts the first 200 characters of body text

Across the 383 snippets, the minimum length was 200 characters, the median 202 and the maximum 206. **100% fell within 200–206 characters, and 100% were truncated at the end.** The cut is made mechanically by character count, regardless of sentence boundaries.

The content makes it obvious these aren't deliberately written descriptions.

| Found in the snippet | Share |
| --- | ---: |
| Runs of capital letters (traces of English headings rendered in uppercase) | 78% |
| Bullets starting with `*` (global navigation) | 20% |
| `Image:` (image alt attributes) | 17% |
| `[Input]` `[Button]` `[Select]` (form elements) | 14% |

A meta description never contains navigation or form elements. This is text taken from the rendered page.

### But it isn't the first 200 characters of the raw page

We tested this directly. We took the first 24 characters of each snippet, searched for them in the raw text of the same page fetched ourselves, and measured the offset where the snippet began.

Of the 12 pages we could match, **0 started at the top (character 0–5)** and **12 had their opening discarded**.

The discarded openings were all of the same kind:

- `Skip to content` / `Skip to main content`
- Rows of global navigation links
- Browser warnings and campaign banners

For example, 1,506 characters of boilerplate were discarded on Semrush's Japanese page, 1,036 on Screaming Frog, and 3,364 on TechRadar; the snippet started in the body text that followed.

**In other words, body extraction (boilerplate removal) happens first, and the first 200 characters of the result are cut.**

`title` is recorded as a separate field, and it matched the start of the snippet in **only 13%** of cases. The title and the snippet are different things.

## The meta description wasn't used

The deciding evidence was a one-word difference.

Screaming Frog's product page lists supported operating systems differently in the meta description and in the body.

The snippet recorded in the conversation log was:

> SCREAMING FROG SEO SPIDER The industry leading website crawler for Windows, macOS and **Linux**, trusted by thousands of SEOs and agencies worldwide for technical SEO site audits. \* Overview \* User Gui…

It contains the body's `Linux`, not the meta description's `Ubuntu`. The leading `SCREAMING FROG SEO SPIDER` is the h1 rendered in uppercase.

We fetched and compared the meta descriptions of five more pages (Rakko Inc., Nobilista, GMO AI Kantan Shukyaku, Ahrefs Webmaster Tools, and Nobilista's pricing page). **None of them had meta description wording in the snippet.**

Nobilista's homepage, for instance, has a 145-character meta description, but its snippet contained the global navigation. GMO AI Kantan Shukyaku's meta description is only 14 characters, and its snippet began with the h1 "集客を「かんたん」に最大化する" ("Maximize customer acquisition, the easy way").

### This differs from Google

Google's official documentation states that it builds snippets from page content and the meta description. **Material Google uses was not being passed to ChatGPT.**

We want to be careful here. We only tested ChatGPT. AI Overviews and Gemini, which build on Google Search's index, may well make use of the meta description. We have not tested them.

The point isn't that "meta descriptions are obsolete." It's that **they don't count as ChatGPT optimization**.

## Changing the query doesn't change the snippet

On the same day, 15 URLs were picked up by different questions and different search queries. For **all 15, the snippets matched exactly**.

In Google's results, the excerpt shown for the same page changes with the query. This ChatGPT field doesn't. **It isn't looking at the query.**

The flip side is that there's no room to tailor snippets per query. There is only one lever: what you put in the first 200 characters.

## Body extraction can fail

What happens across days? Of the 89 snippets that appeared on both days, **73 (82%) matched exactly** and 16 changed.

The query was not the cause.

| Cause of change | Count |
| --- | ---: |
| Relative date display only ("Updated today" → "Updated yesterday") | 2 |
| **Body extraction started at a different point** | 8 |
| The page content itself changed | 6 |

A changing relative date also means **snippets aren't a fixed cache; they're rebuilt on every fetch**. And each rebuild can produce a different extraction result.

The most common cause was the shifted extraction point. Same page, same 200-character slot:

### What the failed pages had in common

Both pages where extraction failed (Nobilista's homepage and Peec AI's comparison page) **had neither `main` nor `article`**.

| Page | Extraction | `main` | `article` |
| --- | --- | :---: | :---: |
| co.nobilista.com | **Failed** | No | No |
| peec.ai (comparison page) | **Failed** | No | No |
| screamingfrog.co.uk | Succeeded | Yes | No |
| rakko.inc | Succeeded | Yes | No |
| kantan.gmo | Succeeded | Yes | No |
| ahrefs.com | Succeeded | No | No |

This is an observation of six pages, so it doesn't show that "adding `main` guarantees success." Ahrefs succeeded without `main`. But the fact we did see is that **both failed pages declared no body region**. That's reason enough to give the extractor more to work with.

### It doesn't necessarily come out verbatim

We measured overlap between the answer text and the set of snippets using runs of 8 consecutive matching characters. It was **6–11%**. **90% of the answer does not match the snippets verbatim.**

That's why keyword stuffing doesn't work. What carried through, as in the three examples above, was **a single sentence shaped as a claim**. The sentence "usable without specialist knowledge" survived with its meaning intact, even after being paraphrased.

## But figures come from the body

Tidying up the first 200 characters isn't the whole job.

The answer about GMO AI Kantan Shukyaku includes the figure "3,840 yen for a single month (excl. tax)." That string **appeared nowhere in the 383 snippets**. When we fetched the press release page shown as the citation, the figure was there in the body, verbatim.

The same answer also says "4,224 yen including tax," which appears in none of the sources. It's 1.1 × 3,840 yen: **a figure the model calculated and added on its own**. The arithmetic is correct, but a number not stated in the source appears in a cited context.

A previous article on this site covered a case where no tool call fetching a page body was recorded at all, and every fact in the answer could be explained by strings inside the snippets. This time that wasn't the case. **Both happen.** The first 200 characters alone aren't enough, and neither is the body alone.

## Stable snippets don't mean stable answers

This is easy to misread, so let's be explicit.

Over the same two days, 73 of 89 snippets matched exactly. But **the answers changed**.

For the question about GEO tools, Profound was listed first on Sep 5, but on Sep 6 Otterly came first, with the flat recommendation "if you pick just one, OtterlyAI." Peec AI, listed on Sep 5, disappeared from the answer and Scrunch took its place.

In a Japanese question with three conditions attached, one of the three slots also switched to a different product. On Rakko Keyword's pricing, Sep 5 said "from 1,320 yen on monthly billing," while Sep 6 said "Entry plan at 660 yen on monthly billing." 660 yen is the official price on annual billing, so the Sep 6 statement is most likely inaccurate.

**Same input, moving output.** Tidying the first 200 characters is work to get you "introduced correctly once you're a candidate," not work that locks in a ranking. Don't judge from a single observation; measure several times, spaced out over time.

## What this observation shows

- All 383 snippets recorded in the conversation JSON were 200–206 characters and truncated at the end
- On all 12 pages we could match, the header, navigation and skip links had been discarded (0 started at the top of the page)
- On the 6 pages checked, no meta description wording appeared
- The 15 URLs picked up by different queries on the same day had exactly matching snippets
- Across days, 82% matched, and the main cause of change was a shift in the body extraction point
- Both pages where extraction failed had neither `main` nor `article`

**The sample is 5 prompts × 2 days, and we didn't look at anything other than ChatGPT. Since Google states that it uses the meta description, Google's products could show the opposite result.**

## How to check your own pages

1. Turn off memory, clear custom instructions, and open a new chat
2. Ask a question in your category and capture the conversation JSON **after the answer has finished**
3. Confirm there's no `model_editable_context` message (if there is, memory is active)
4. Find your domain's `snippet` in `search_result_groups[].entries[]`
5. Compare it against your page's meta description. It probably won't match
6. Look at what's at the start of the snippet. If it's navigation, body extraction failed

If you enter a URL into our page audit tool, it shows **the first 200 characters of body text with the header, navigation and footer removed**, as is. For pages that don't declare a body region, it flags the risk that extraction will be replaced by navigation.

Note that the audit reads the HTML the server returns, and we haven't confirmed whether ChatGPT sees the page after JavaScript execution. The extraction algorithm isn't the same either. Use it as a guide.

## FAQ

### Does that mean I can stop writing meta descriptions?

No. Google officially states that it builds snippets from page content and the meta description, so it matters for how you appear in Google Search. The only thing this observation shows is that meta description wording didn't appear in the snippets recorded in ChatGPT's conversation logs. The takeaway is to stop counting meta description work as ChatGPT optimization.

### Is it the same for Gemini or AI Overviews?

We don't know; we haven't tested them. If anything, since AI Overviews builds on Google Search's index, the meta description is likely to be used. Don't extend conclusions about ChatGPT to AI search in general.

### What should I put in the first 200 characters?

One sentence stating what your service does. The sentences that survived into answers this time were shaped as claims, such as "usable without specialist knowledge," "greatly helps extract user needs," and "find and fix 170+ common SEO issues." Keyword lists didn't survive. Verbatim overlap between answers and snippets was 6–11%, so the text doesn't come out as-is.

### How do I keep navigation out of the snippet?

Normally it's removed automatically: on all 12 pages we could match, the header and skip links had been discarded. The problem is when removal fails, and both pages that failed this time declared no body region. What you can do now is wrap the body in `main` or `article` and mark up the header, navigation and footer as `header` / `nav` / `footer` elements. Note that `data-nosnippet` is an attribute for controlling Google's snippets, a separate mechanism from what we observed. We haven't confirmed whether ChatGPT respects it.

### Are pages with empty snippets at a disadvantage?

Not necessarily. This time, the 24 citations shown in the most prominent form within answers (a link right under a heading) **all had empty snippets**. Pages with snippets ended up as smaller citations placed alongside. What the presence or absence of a snippet means hasn't been disclosed, and it shouldn't be read as better or worse.

### Is checking once enough?

No. This time, 82% of snippets matched across the two days, yet the order of tools in the answer shifted, one dropped out and another came in. In one case, the quoted price changed for the same question. Measure several times, spaced out over time, and record the results as ratios.
